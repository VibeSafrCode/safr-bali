# C1 Visa for Indonesia: how to stay in Bali for up to 180 days

Type: single-entry C1 visitor visa. Initial stay: up to 60 days. First extension: up to another 60 days. Second extension: up to another 60 days. Maximum with extensions: up to 180 days. A standard extension does not require an exit.

## Key facts

- Type: single-entry C1 visitor visa.
- Initial stay: up to 60 days.
- Maximum with extensions: up to 180 days.
- Number of entries: one.
- No sponsor is required for a standard C1.
- Proof of funds: at least the equivalent of USD 2,000.
- Passport validity: at least 6 months.
- SAFRWAY price: IDR 2,000,000 (≈ {{USD}}).

The C1 can be a good fit if you want to spend several months in Indonesia but do not want to apply for a [KITAS](/bali/knowledge/kitas-indonesia/) immediately.

After entering on a C1, you can stay for up to 60 days. The stay can then be extended to 120 days and, after a second extension, to 180 days.

The C1 remains single-entry. If you leave Indonesia, you cannot use the same C1 for another entry.

## Who is the C1 for?

Consider the C1 if you:

- want to spend 2–6 months in Bali;
- want an initial stay of up to 60 days;
- want the option to extend up to 180 days without leaving Indonesia;
- do not plan to work for an Indonesian company;
- do not want to apply for a KITAS immediately;
- want an option between a short VOA/eVOA stay and a longer-term residence status.

If you already know you will stay for more than 180 days, compare the C1 with [D12](/bali/visas/d12/) and [E33G](/bali/visas/e33g/) before applying.

## How long does the C1 last?

The initial stay is up to 60 days from entry.

The standard cycle is:

| Stage | Total stay |
|---|---:|
| Initial entry | up to 60 days |
| After first extension | up to 120 days |
| After second extension | up to 180 days |

One C1 stay cycle can therefore reach up to 180 days.

## C1 is single-entry

This point matters.

An extension increases the length of the current stay, but it does not turn the C1 into a multiple-entry visa.

Once you leave Indonesia, the current C1 cycle ends. A new entry requires a new visa or another suitable status.

## What documents are required?

For a standard C1, you normally need:

- a passport valid for at least 6 months;
- a bank statement covering the last 3 months;
- proof of funds equivalent to at least USD 2,000;
- a recent color photograph.

Additional documents can be requested in specific cases.

## Is a sponsor required?

For a standard [C1](/bali/visas/c1/), a sponsor is currently not required.

An applicant can apply through their own eVisa account.

If you do not want to handle the application and document preparation yourself, SAFRWAY can manage the preparation and support.

## How do extensions work?

After the initial 60 days, the C1 can be extended for another 60 days.

A second extension can then add up to another 60 days.

The sequence is:

60 → 120 → 180 days.

A standard extension within the current stay cycle does not require leaving Indonesia.

Read more: [Extend your C1 in Bali for another 60 days](/bali/visas/c1/extension/).

## Can you work on a C1?

The C1 is a visitor visa, not a work status.

It is not intended for receiving salary or payment from Indonesian individuals or companies.

If your situation involves remote employment with a foreign employer and long-term residence, look at [E33G](/bali/visas/e33g/) separately.

## How is a C1 different from a KITAS?

A C1 can support a stay of several months, but it is not a [KITAS](/bali/knowledge/kitas-indonesia/).

A KITAS is a different immigration status and is usually more suitable for people building a longer-term life in Indonesia.

The difference can affect more than stay length; it can also matter for practical areas such as banking, documents and other local services.

Think of the C1 as a useful several-month solution rather than a full substitute for a KITAS.

## SIM card and phone use on a C1

The C1 itself does not prevent a foreign national from registering a local SIM using a passport.

SIM registration and device IMEI rules are separate issues.

If your phone was bought outside Indonesia and you want to use it with an Indonesian SIM for a longer period, IMEI rules need to be considered separately.

This matters for C1 holders because a full C1 cycle can last up to 180 days.

## What happens after 180 days?

After reaching the maximum stay under the current C1, you need to leave Indonesia and choose the next immigration scenario.

Depending on your plans, you can:

- apply for a new C1 and start a new cycle;
- consider D12;
- check whether you meet E33G requirements;
- choose another suitable visa or KITAS status.

You do not have to decide alone; message a SAFRWAY manager and compare the options.

## When is the C1 especially convenient?

The C1 can be convenient if you need up to roughly half a year and want to:

- enter with an initial stay of up to 60 days;
- avoid a visa run after just 30 days;
- have the option to extend up to 180 days;
- avoid moving directly to a KITAS.

For a short trip, eVOA/VOA may be simpler. For longer-term residence, another status may fit better.

## FAQ

### Can I stay for 180 days continuously on a C1?

Yes, if you complete two extension stages after the initial 60 days.

### Do I have to leave after the first 60 days?

No, if you extend your stay.

### Can I use the same C1 for a second entry?

No. The [C1](/bali/visas/c1/) is single-entry.

### Can I register a local SIM on a C1?

The C1 itself does not prevent it. Foreign nationals can register a SIM using a passport. Device IMEI rules are a separate issue.

### Is a C1 a KITAS?

No. A C1 is a visitor visa. A KITAS is a different immigration status.

### Can I apply for the C1 myself?

A standard C1 can be applied for through eVisa. If you prefer not to manage the process yourself, SAFRWAY can handle preparation and support.
