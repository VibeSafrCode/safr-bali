# Certified and Sworn Document Translations in Indonesia

## Quick answer

**When an authority, bank or other organization requests an official translation, you need to check not only the language but also the required form of certification.** In Indonesia, the status of a sworn translator, **penerjemah tersumpah**, is regulated by law. SAFRWAY helps verify the requirements and arrange a suitable translation through a specialist.

Translation for everyday communication and translation for submission to a public authority are different tasks. Even a high-quality ordinary translation may be unsuitable if the recipient requires the signature or seal of a translator with formally recognized status.

## Documents we can assess

- Passport and personal records, birth and marriage certificates and official letters, subject to the receiving authority’s rules.
- Diplomas, transcripts and other education records.

- Incorporation documents, company deeds and resolutions, extracts and representatives’ powers of attorney.
- Contracts, letters and other documents requiring a specific translation format.

## What is a sworn translator in Indonesia?

Under **Permenkum No. 4/2025**, an Indonesian *penerjemah tersumpah* is a translator appointed, sworn in and registered through the prescribed process by Indonesia’s Ministry of Law. The status should therefore not be attributed to any agency or professional merely because they know a language.

For documents presented **in another country**, certified translation, sworn translation and notarial certification may have different meanings. An Indonesian sworn translation does not guarantee automatic acceptance abroad. We check the receiving organization’s required format first.

## When translation must be combined with other certification

Translation may be part of a document process that also includes an [apostille](/en/bali/documents/apostille/), notarization or [consular legalization](/en/bali/documents/legalization/), but those procedures are not interchangeable. If an apostille is requested for a translation or related instrument, we check whether that form is eligible and whose official capacity is actually being authenticated.

For [corporate documents](/en/bali/documents/corporate/), only selected pages or extracts may need translation. We do not automatically recommend translating a company’s entire historical archive.

## What to clarify before ordering

1. Source language and required target language.
2. Document category, size and legibility.
3. Country and organization where it will be used.
4. Whether a sworn translator, notary, original stamp or hard copy is required.
5. Submission deadline and need for physical delivery.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a translation I made myself be certified?

This depends on the recipient’s requirements and the procedure available from the particular authorized professional. We cannot promise that someone else’s finished translation will simply be signed without checking and appropriate professional work.

### Is sworn translation compulsory for every visa?

No. Requirements differ by visa, country and document type. We review the requirements for your case.

### Can an ordinary notarized translation replace a sworn translation?

Not always. Notarial authentication and a translator’s official status are different matters; the receiving authority determines the acceptable format.

### Can I get a translation without sending the original?

A scan is often enough for assessment, but whether the completed translation can be issued depends on the document and the translator’s or recipient’s rules.

### Can you translate documents from Russian into Indonesian?

We can check whether a suitable specialist is available and which format the recipient will accept. We confirm availability before the order.

### Are apostille and delivery included?

Not unless expressly included in your individual quote. [Apostille and legalization](/en/bali/knowledge/documents/apostille-vs-legalization/) are separate procedures.
