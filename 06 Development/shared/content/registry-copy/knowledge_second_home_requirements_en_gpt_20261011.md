# Second Home E33: Financial Guarantee and Document Requirements

## Quick Answer

**Proving a high income or showing a villa rental agreement is not sufficient for Second Home E33.** The official proof-of-funds procedure provides for a specific immigration financial guarantee: funds held in an eligible account at an Indonesian state-owned bank or a qualifying property-based alternative.

The applicable method and the exact set of required documents must be confirmed before applying.

For application assistance, see our [Second Home E33](/bali/visas/second-home/) service page.

## Bank Funds Option: USD 130,000

According to a page published in 2026 by the Jakarta Barat Immigration Office, the Second Home proof-of-funds procedure requires a bank confirmation and an account statement from one of the specified Indonesian state-owned banks, such as **BRI, BNI, BTN, Mandiri or BSI**.

The total amount of funds must be **at least USD 130,000 or the equivalent**.

This amount represents **a condition of the immigration financial guarantee**, not a government application fee or a payment to SAFRWAY.

You should not transfer these funds to a visa agency or assume that information displayed in any banking application automatically constitutes acceptable evidence of compliance.

The bank account and the supporting documents must meet the requirements of the applicable procedure.

## Property Option: From USD 1,000,000

The same official procedure describes an alternative based on property ownership.

The applicant must provide ownership documentation for a qualifying **flat or apartment** in Indonesia, with an established value of **at least USD 1,000,000 or the equivalent**.

The ownership must be supported by the required documents from the relevant **BPN** authority.

This does not mean that **any** villa, plot of land, property under construction, long-term lease or agreement with a property developer automatically qualifies.

The following matters must be checked:

- The type of property right.
- The documents relating to the property.
- Compliance with the requirements governing foreign ownership.
- Whether the immigration authorities will accept the submitted documents.

Simply having accommodation in Bali does not automatically entitle a person to residence status.

## When Must the Financial Obligation Be Fulfilled?

Official guidance for certain long-term residence categories provides for a period in which immigration-related financial obligations must be fulfilled after obtaining ITAS.

For standard Second Home, a period of **90 days** is frequently mentioned.

However, both the starting point for calculating this period and the applicable procedure must be checked against the current application.

**The reference to 90 days must not be interpreted as a promise that the visa will be processed within 90 days.**

Failure to fulfil the financial obligation may have consequences for the validity of the applicant's immigration status.

Verification of compliance is a separate and important stage of the process.

## What Is Checked in the Documents?

The review covers the following matters:

- **Applicant's identity and status:** the applicant's identity and current immigration status.
- **Application and obligations:** the application itself and the obligations prescribed under the relevant procedure.
- **Bank evidence:** the authenticity of the bank confirmation, the ownership of the account and the type of banking institution.
- **Property evidence:** alternatively, the property documents and the legally established type of ownership right.
- **Consistency of information:** whether the dates, amounts and applicant's personal details correspond across the documents.
- **Additional requirements:** any additional conditions arising from the involvement of a sponsor or representatives.

If corporate or notarised documentation is required, see our [corporate documents](/bali/documents/corporate/) and [certified translations](/bali/documents/certified-translations/) pages.

## E33 Is Different from Investor KITAS and Silver Hair

[Investor KITAS E28A](/bali/visas/investor-kitas/) is associated with requirements concerning a foreign investor's participation in a company.

The retirement category [E33F](/bali/visas/retirement-kitas/) and E33E Silver Hair have their own separate eligibility criteria.

**Do not transfer financial amounts or other requirements from one category to another**, even when all these options are being considered for long-term residence in Indonesia.

Each category must be assessed according to its own applicable conditions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is USD 130,000 the Price of the Visa?

No.

It is a published financial-proof threshold associated with the immigration guarantee.

SAFRWAY's assistance fee and any applicable government charges are determined separately.

### Can I Prove the Funds Are Held in Any Foreign Bank?

The official procedure specifies an eligible account at an Indonesian state-owned bank.

An account at another bank should not automatically be assumed to qualify.

### Does a Villa Under a Long-Term Lease Qualify?

A lease agreement cannot be treated as equivalent to the required ownership documentation for an eligible property.

### Can I Use a Family Member's Funds?

The published procedure refers to property or an account associated with the applicant.

Any non-standard ownership arrangement requires an individual assessment and cannot be guaranteed to qualify.

### How Long Do I Have to Provide the Financial Proof?

The applicable requirement and the starting point for calculating the deadline must be checked for the selected category.

The frequently mentioned **90-day period** is not a universal deadline for every visa category or other procedure.

### Does Investing in Property Give Me the Right to Work?

There is no basis for treating a property investment as automatically granting permission to work.

Employment and other work activities are subject to separate immigration and labour requirements.
