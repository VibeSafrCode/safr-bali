# Closing a PT PMA in Indonesia: Company Liquidation

## Quick answer

**Closing a PT PMA normally involves a corporate decision, a liquidation process and the formal termination of legal-entity status.** Cancelling an NIB or ceasing to earn revenue does not, by itself, dissolve the company.

If a PT PMA is no longer needed, it cannot be considered closed merely because it no longer earns income, rents an office or uses its NIB. **Stopping business operations and ending the company's legal existence are different stages.**

## When company closure may be needed

- The company has stopped trading and its owners wish to terminate its legal presence in Indonesia.
- The project is ending following an asset sale or the termination of contracts.
- The company needs to be formally dissolved, rather than simply left inactive in OSS.
- The company is dormant but continues to have reporting, tax or corporate obligations.

## Why cancelling an NIB is not enough

Indonesia's general company law provides for formal **pembubaran** (dissolution) and, as applicable, **likuidasi** (liquidation). During liquidation, liabilities, notices and the disposition of assets must be addressed. Completion of the company's legal status is recorded through the prescribed procedure, not by a single OSS action.

## What must be checked before starting

- Whether the company has bank accounts, funds, assets or ongoing contracts.
- Whether there are debts, creditors, employees, unpaid liabilities or tax issues.
- The state of corporate documents, reports and AHU/OSS data.
- Existing sectoral licences and any other permits.
- Foreign KITAS holders whose status is connected to the company.
- The applicable legal ground for dissolution and who is authorised to act.

## How the process may proceed

1. **Initial review.** Examine the company, obligations and potential obstacles to closure.
2. **Corporate stage.** Authorised persons adopt the necessary decisions; documentation is prepared with a notary.
3. **AHU and notifications.** Register the start of the procedure as required; appoint a liquidator if applicable.
4. **Settlement.** Deal with assets, creditors, employees, taxes and other obligations as necessary.
5. **Completion of liquidation.** Prepare the final documents and formally conclude legal-entity status under the relevant rules.
6. **Related registrations.** Separately review OSS/NIB, tax and banking records within the agreed service scope.

The exact sequence depends on the legal ground for closure and the company's current condition. It should not be understood as a promise to remove every record immediately.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I close a PT PMA that has no revenue?

A lack of revenue does not replace formal closure. Outstanding obligations and the available liquidation procedure must be assessed.

### Is it enough to delete the NIB or end the office lease?

No. Neither action terminates the legal entity's status.

### Must creditors be notified and tax matters settled?

Depending on the legal ground and condition of the company, yes. Procedural requirements and actual obligations must be reviewed before closure.

### Can a company with debts be closed?

This requires an individual legal assessment. Ordinary closure cannot be promised without addressing liabilities.

### What happens to KITAS when the company closes?

Where immigration status depends on the company, the consequences must be assessed separately **before** liquidation actions. Closure does not guarantee that a KITAS will remain valid.
