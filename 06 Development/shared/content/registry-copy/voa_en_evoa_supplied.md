## Who is the eVOA for?

Consider the eVOA if you:

- are traveling to Bali or another part of Indonesia for a short stay;
- want to arrange the visa before departure;
- want an initial stay of up to 30 days;
- want the option to extend for another 30 days;
- want fewer steps to deal with after landing;
- do not need a long-term visa from the start.

If you already know you will stay for several months, compare the eVOA with C1 and other longer-term options.

## eVOA vs VOA at the airport

Both are Visa on Arrival and give the same basic stay period.

The practical difference is when you arrange them:

- **eVOA** — online before the trip;
- **VOA** — after arrival in Indonesia.

### If you get a standard VOA after landing

At the airport you need to complete a separate visa process:

1. find the Visa on Arrival area;
2. pay at the airport bank/payment counter;
3. receive the payment confirmation or voucher;
4. then continue with visa processing and immigration control.

During busy arrival waves, queues can become significant. In peak situations, practical traveler and airport-service reports describe the combined visa/immigration process taking up to around 2–3 hours. This is not an official average, but a peak-case scenario.

If you also need to exchange cash after arrival, that can add another stop, and airport exchange rates are not always the most favorable.

### If your eVOA is ready in advance

You arrive with the visa already issued.

You do **not need** to buy a VOA or collect a payment voucher after landing.

If your passport, eVOA and entry point meet current autogate requirements, you may be able to pass immigration through automated gates instead of the regular officer queue.

That is why the eVOA can make the arrival journey much simpler.

After entry:

- up to 30 days — initial stay;
- up to 60 days — after one extension.

The 30-day stay itself starts from the actual date of entry.
