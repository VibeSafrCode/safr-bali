# Apostille or Consular Legalization: Which Process Does Your Document Need?

## Quick answer

**An apostille and consular legalization are different methods of authenticating the origin of official documents for use abroad.** Apostilles apply to eligible documents between countries where the 1961 Convention operates in the relevant relationship. Other cases may require a different authentication process, sometimes involving a foreign ministry and consulate. [Document translation](/en/bali/documents/certified-translations/) is yet another, separate task.

Problems start when a procedure is chosen solely by destination country. You must check the issuing country, legal category of the document and the requirements of the organization receiving it.

## Comparison of the key questions

| Question | Apostille | Consular legalization |
|---|---|---|
| When does it apply? | When the Convention is operative between the relevant states and covers the document | When the apostille is inapplicable or a different mandatory procedure applies |
| Who authenticates it? | Competent authority in the document’s country of origin | Authorized bodies along the applicable chain, sometimes including the foreign ministry and a consulate |
| What does it confirm? | The signature, signatory’s official capacity and official seal or stamp | The official origin of signatures and seals at specified stages |
| Does it verify the content? | No | Generally not; it is not a substitute for checking contents or validity |
| Does it replace translation? | No | No |
| Is the process universal for every document? | No, exceptions exist | No; it depends on document, authority and country |

## Scenario 1. Indonesian certificate for another country

If a document was issued in Indonesia and the receiving state accepts apostilles, an [AHU apostille assessment](/en/bali/documents/apostille/) may be appropriate. First, check whether the copy is the correct official record, whether prior authentication is necessary and whether translation is required.

Since December 2025, Indonesia’s apostille procedure has been governed by **Permenkum No. 50/2025**. In June 2026, AHU announced the new application service at **layanan.ahu.go.id**. Older articles may describe an obsolete interface.

## Scenario 2. A document issued in Russia for use in Indonesia

Russia is the issuing country, so the competent Russian apostille authority should be checked rather than attempting to obtain an Indonesian apostille for the Russian original. Next, establish the Indonesian recipient’s requirements for the document category, language, acceptable translation and recency of the certificate.

Different document types and offices may reach different outcomes. This example is not a universal checklist for all visas, registrations and transactions.

## Scenario 3. Commercial or tax document

The Convention excludes certain administrative documents dealing directly with commercial or customs operations. Indonesia’s Kemlu also identifies excluded categories of business and tax documents. **Not every company document is automatically excluded**, however: a notarial corporate deed should be evaluated by its legal character, not simply by its intended business use.

In this situation, compare [corporate document preparation](/en/bali/documents/corporate/) with the [consular legalization route](/en/bali/documents/legalization/). Promising an apostille for every company document is legally unreliable.

## Scenario 4. The document has already been translated

Translation does not turn a private document into a public instrument or replace official authentication. **Permenkum No. 4/2025** governs the status of sworn translators in Indonesia. For use in another country, a recipient may demand a different form of certified or sworn translation.

The authenticity of the translator’s signature, the authenticity of the original document and the accuracy of translated content are separate issues.

## How to identify the first step yourself

1. Write down the **country of issuance**.
2. Identify the **country and organization** receiving the document.
3. Determine the **document category**: personal, official, notarial, corporate, tax or commercial.
4. Check whether the Convention applies in the relevant relationship and whether the document is excluded.
5. Confirm the recipient’s requirements for **language, original or copy, and recency**.
6. Only then select translation, apostille, [legalization](/en/bali/documents/legalization/) or another process.

This approach reduces unnecessary expenses but does not replace a case-specific review with the receiving authority.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are an apostille and notarization the same thing?

No. A notarial act may create or authenticate the relevant instrument, while an apostille authenticates the origin of a signature or seal under the Convention.

### Can any contract receive an apostille?

There is no universal answer. You must determine what was authenticated, whether the record qualifies as a public or notarial document and whether an exclusion applies.

### Does an apostille remove the expiry requirements for certificates?

No. An apostille certificate itself generally has no separate expiration date, but the recipient may require a recently issued underlying certificate or currently valid document.

### Is an apostille sufficient without translation?

Sometimes yes, sometimes no. The recipient sets translation requirements.

### If a document already has an apostille, must it be taken to an embassy again?

For documents properly covered by the Convention in the relevant relationship, routine additional diplomatic or consular legalization is not required. Separate format and translation requirements may remain.

### What if an authority asks for consular legalization?

Clarify the document category and the basis for that request, then check the permissible process. [SAFRWAY Documents](/en/bali/documents/) can coordinate an initial assessment and specialized support.
