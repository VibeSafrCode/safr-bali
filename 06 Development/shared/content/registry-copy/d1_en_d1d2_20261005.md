# D1 Multiple Entry — a tourist visa for regular visits to Indonesia

If you visit Bali regularly rather than just once a year — for the winter, to see family, for several longer trips, or simply to keep a valid visa without applying again before each return — D1 may be more convenient than C1 or eVOA.

The main idea behind D1:

**one multiple-entry visa → several trips to Indonesia → up to 60 days on each new entry.**

When needed, the current stay can be extended under the rules in force.

## Who is D1 for?

Consider D1 if you:

- return to Indonesia regularly;
- spend several periods in Bali during the year;
- visit family or friends;
- do not want to apply for eVOA or C1 each time;
- want a multiple-entry visa valid for a longer period;
- travel around Asia and return to Indonesia periodically;
- attend particular events, meetings, exhibitions or conferences as a participant, provided your purpose remains within the scope of a visitor visa.

D1 is particularly useful for **regular repeat trips**, rather than one long, uninterrupted period of residence.

## How do the 60 days per entry work?

It is important to distinguish two things:

### The validity of the visa itself

For example, D1 can be issued for 1 or 2 years.

This is the period during which you can use the visa for repeat trips.

### The permitted stay after a particular entry

A new period of stay begins with each entry.

Initially — **up to 60 days**.

A 1-year visa therefore does not mean you can remain in Indonesia continuously for 1 year without an extension or departure.

The correct way to understand it:

**a longer visa validity → a separate period of stay for each entry.**

## Can D1 be extended?

Yes.

Under current rules, the period of stay granted under D1 can be extended.

Initially, you receive up to **60 days** from the date of entry.

The current visit can then be extended, with the total length of that stay reaching **up to 180 days**.

### Extension price through SAFRWAY

**2 500 000 IDR (≈ {{USD_2_5M}})**

Important:

it is the **current period of stay** that is extended, not the overall validity of your multiple-entry visa.

For example:

- you enter on D1;
- receive 60 days;
- extend that visit;
- later leave;
- then enter again on the same, still-valid D1;
- a new period of stay begins after the new entry.

## What can you do on D1?

The main purpose of D1 is tourism and personal travel.

Within the permitted scope of visitor activities, it is suitable for:

- tourism;
- holidays;
- travel in Bali and other parts of Indonesia;
- family visits;
- visiting friends;
- attending particular meetings, incentive programmes, conventions and exhibitions as a participant;
- transit or onward travel.

If your main purpose is business negotiations, regular business meetings, checking goods or signing commercial agreements, [D2](/bali/visas/d2/) is the more appropriate option to consider.

## What is not permitted on D1?

D1 is a visitor visa.

It does not give you the right to:

- take up employment in Indonesia;
- receive a local salary;
- receive payment for work from an Indonesian company or individual;
- use a tourist visa as a substitute for a work KITAS;
- sell goods or services in Indonesia as though you held work authorisation.

If you live in Bali and work remotely for a foreign company, you can separately check whether [E33G Remote Worker KITAS](/bali/visas/e33g/) is suitable.

## D1 or C1?

Both categories can serve tourism and personal purposes, but they work differently.

### C1

- single entry;
- initially up to 60 days;
- extensions possible up to 180 days;
- the current visa ends when you leave;
- useful for one longer trip.

### D1

- multiple entries;
- initially up to 60 days per entry;
- the current stay can be extended;
- the visa remains valid after a trip until its overall validity expires;
- useful for regular returns.

For one long season in Bali, [C1](/bali/visas/c1/) is often simpler.

If you plan **several returns**, D1 can be significantly more convenient.

## D1 or eVOA?

[eVOA](/bali/visas/voa/) is usually suitable for short trips:

- 30 days;
- one extension for another 30 days;
- a new trip requires a new visa arrangement.

D1 is suitable when you already know you will visit Indonesia regularly for a year or longer.

## D1 or D2?

In brief:

**D1 — tourism and personal visits.**

**D2 — regular business visits.**

Both categories:

- allow multiple entries;
- provide an initial stay of up to 60 days per entry;
- can be extended under current rules;
- do not authorise local employment.

If you are unsure which category fits, use the [D1 and D2 comparison](/bali/visas/d1-d2/) or send the manager a description of your trip’s purpose.

## What documents are required?

The basic preparation for D1 includes:

- an international passport valid for at least 6 months;
- bank statements for the last 3 months;
- proof of funds equivalent to at least 2 000 USD;
- a recent colour photograph;
- a CV;
- a travel itinerary;
- evidence of the trip’s purpose and, where required, sponsor / penjamin documents;
- any additional evidence required for the particular application.

SAFRWAY checks the exact list before filing, because eVisa requirements may change.

Read more: [D1/D2 documents](/bali/knowledge/d1-d2/documents/).

## Is a sponsor required?

The dedicated official D1 page states that a sponsor / penjamin is generally not required for an application. There are exceptions for certain nationalities.

However, not needing a sponsor does not remove the requirement for supporting documents about the purpose of the trip. SAFRWAY checks in advance:

- whether your particular case requires a sponsor;
- which documents the applicant must provide;
- what information is needed for filing;
- the available visa validity period;
- the current eVisa requirements.

We determine the applicable process based on your nationality and documents before filing.

## Can D1 be converted to KITAS without leaving?

You should not rely on this as the standard route.

Current official Immigration information states that D1/D2 stays can be extended, but the visitor stay permit **cannot be converted directly to ITAS**.

SAFRWAY therefore does not promise D1 → KITAS without departure simply because some agencies offer such a service.

If your ultimate goal is KITAS, contact the manager in advance. We will check current rules and suggest a lawful route for your circumstances.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is D1 a multiple-entry visa?

Yes.

### How long can I stay in Indonesia after one entry?

Initially, up to 60 days.

### Can I extend my stay?

Yes. Under current rules, that visit can be extended to a total of up to 180 days.

### Is D1 cancelled when I leave?

No, provided the multiple-entry visa itself remains valid.

### Do I get a new stay period when I re-enter?

Yes. A new entry starts a new period of stay under the valid visa.

### Can I work in Bali on D1?

No. D1 is a visitor visa.

### Can I receive a salary from an Indonesian company?

No.

### Can I switch from D1 to KITAS inside Indonesia?

You should not rely on direct conversion. Current official information does not allow direct D1/D2 conversion to ITAS. The available route must be checked when you contact us.

### What is the difference between D1 and C1?

C1 is single-entry and useful for one longer trip. D1 is multiple-entry and useful for repeat trips.
