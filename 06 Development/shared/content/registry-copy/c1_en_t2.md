# C1 Visa for Indonesia — up to 180 days in Bali

If you plan to spend several months in Bali, the C1 is one of the clearest options to consider.

The initial stay is up to 60 days in Indonesia. You can then extend the stay, and the total period for one entry can reach up to 180 days.

**C1 application through SAFRWAY — IDR 2,000,000 (≈ {{USD}}).**

We check your documents, prepare the application, support the process and send you the approved electronic visa once it is issued.

## Who is the C1 for?

A C1 can make sense if you:

- plan to stay in Indonesia for several weeks or months;
- want an initial stay of up to 60 days;
- want the option to extend your stay up to 180 days without leaving Indonesia;
- do not want to apply for a KITAS immediately;
- are visiting for tourism, a personal trip or another permitted visitor purpose.

The C1 is a single-entry visa. Once you leave Indonesia, the current C1 cycle ends.

## What documents are needed?

For a standard C1, the basic set includes:

- a passport valid for at least 6 months;
- a bank statement covering the last 3 months;
- proof of funds equivalent to at least USD 2,000;
- a recent color photograph.

If your case requires an additional document, we will tell you during the application review.

## Is a sponsor required?

For a standard C1, a sponsor is currently not required.

An applicant can use their own eVisa account, while SAFRWAY handles preparation and application support.

Additional requirements can apply to specific categories of applicants; we review those cases separately.

## How long can you stay on a C1?

The standard timeline is:

- up to 60 days — initial stay;
- up to 120 days — after the first extension;
- up to 180 days — after the second extension.

Extensions are handled after entry and paid separately.

If you already have a C1 and need the next stage, use the SAFRWAY C1 extension service.

## Price

**C1 application through SAFRWAY — IDR 2,000,000 (≈ {{USD}}).**

If you later need an extension, each stage is handled separately.

## Important to know

The C1 is a visitor visa, not a KITAS.

It works well for a longer stay without obtaining a residence permit, but it does not provide the same administrative options as a KITAS.

The C1 is also not a work visa and is not intended for receiving salary or payment from Indonesian individuals or companies.

## If you plan to stay in Bali longer than 180 days

After reaching the maximum stay under the current C1, you need a new immigration scenario.

Depending on your plans, you may consider a new C1, D12, E33G or another suitable status.

If you are unsure which route fits your situation, message a SAFRWAY manager and we will help you compare the available options.

## FAQ

### How long is the initial C1 stay?

Up to 60 days from the date of entry.

### Can the C1 be extended?

Yes. With extensions, the total stay can reach up to 180 days.

### Do I need to leave Indonesia for an extension?

No, not for a standard extension within the current stay cycle.

### Is the C1 multiple-entry?

No. The C1 is single-entry. Once you leave Indonesia, the same C1 cannot be used for another entry.

### Is a sponsor required?

For a standard C1, a sponsor is currently not required. Specific applicant categories can have additional requirements.

### Can I work on a C1?

The C1 is not a work visa and is not intended for receiving salary or payment from Indonesian individuals or companies.
