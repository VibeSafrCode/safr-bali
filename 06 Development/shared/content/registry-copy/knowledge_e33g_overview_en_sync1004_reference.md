# E33G Remote Worker KITAS: who it suits and what the requirements are

Type: Limited Stay Visa / ITAS for a remote worker. Duration: 1 year. Remote work for a company outside Indonesia. Verifiable income: at least USD 60,000 per year. Employment agreement with a company outside Indonesia.

## Key facts

- Type: Limited Stay Visa / ITAS for a remote worker.
- Duration: 1 year.
- Remote work for a company outside Indonesia.
- Verifiable income: at least USD 60,000 per year.
- No sponsor is required for a standard E33G.
- Exit and re-entry while the re-entry permit is valid.
- The official requirement is a statement for the last 3 months.
- SAFRWAY additionally requests 12 months of bank statements based on practical visa-issuance experience.
- E33G extension is available online.

E33G is one of the main options for people who want to live in Bali long-term while continuing to work for a company outside Indonesia.

It is not a tourist or visitor visa.

After entry, E33G provides a one-year ITAS and allows you to leave Indonesia and return during the validity of your status, provided your re-entry permit remains valid.

## Who is E33G for?

Consider E33G if the following conditions apply together:

- you work remotely;
- your employer is registered outside Indonesia;
- your working relationship can be documented;
- annual income is at least USD 60,000;
- the income is visible in your banking history;
- you want to live in Indonesia for around a year rather than a few weeks or months;
- you need to leave and return without beginning a new visa cycle.

A difference from one of these standard points does not always mean an automatic refusal.

## The key requirement: a foreign employer

E33G is designed for remote employees of a company outside Indonesia.

Your documents should make clear:

- who the employer is;
- where the company is registered;
- your position;
- when you started working;
- your income;
- that the work is carried out remotely;
- that the documents do not contradict each other.

If the agreement states one figure, the bank statement another and the rest of the documents a third, resolve those inconsistencies before filing.

## What income is required?

The main financial criterion is:

**at least USD 60,000 per year.**

That is approximately USD 5,000 per month.

But the review is not just about arithmetic.

Your income must be verifiable.

The annual amount should therefore be consistent with:

- your employment agreement;
- incoming bank payments;
- employer information;
- any other documents used in your particular case.

## Bank statement: 3 months or 12 months?

Officially, a bank statement for the last 3 months is required. We additionally request a 12-month statement based on practical experience with visa issuance.

It is important to distinguish the official minimum from the way SAFRWAY prepares a case.

### Official reference

Current official E33G requirements list at least **3 months** of bank statements and funds equivalent to at least **USD 2,000**.

### SAFRWAY preparation

When preparing E33G through SAFRWAY, we ask for the last **12 months** of bank statements.

This helps us:

- see income stability;
- check actual incoming payments;
- compare payments with the contract;
- identify lengthy periods without income;
- find inconsistencies before filing.

Thus, **3 months is the official minimum reference**, while **12 months is the additional history requested for SAFRWAY preparation**.

## What documents are needed?

For a basic case, prepare:

- a passport valid for at least 6 months;
- bank statements;
- proof of funds;
- proof of annual income;
- an employment agreement with a company outside Indonesia;
- a recent colour photograph;
- a CV;
- trip information or an itinerary.

Individual cases may require a wider set of documents.

We cover this in the [E33G documents guide](/bali/knowledge/e33g/documents/).

## What should be checked in the employment agreement?

The contract is one of the key documents.

It should not contain formal inconsistencies with your actual situation.

We usually check:

- the company's full name;
- country of registration;
- the employee's full name;
- position;
- employment start date;
- remote-work arrangement;
- income amount and currency;
- payment frequency;
- signatures;
- consistency with incoming bank payments.

A weak existing agreement does not necessarily mean E33G is unavailable.

Show it to the manager first. Sometimes the documentation can be properly updated before filing.

## Is a sponsor required?

No.

A standard E33G does not require an Indonesian sponsor or penjamin.

This makes the application structure simpler than categories that require a separate sponsor.

## What does an E33G holder receive?

After entry, the holder receives an e-ITAS and re-entry permit.

In practice, this provides:

- lawful long-term stay;
- the ability to leave and return;
- no need for visa runs every few months;
- a more practical administrative basis for life in Indonesia.

E33G leads to [KITAS / ITAS](/bali/knowledge/kitas-indonesia/), not a visitor visa.

## Can you work in Indonesia?

E33G is intended for work for a company outside Indonesia.

It is not a general authorisation to:

- work for an Indonesian company;
- receive a salary from an Indonesian employer;
- carry out any local commercial activity without another appropriate basis.

If your work involves an Indonesian business or employer, a different status should be selected.

## Can you leave the country?

Yes.

With a valid re-entry permit, you can leave Indonesia and return.

This is a fundamental difference from single-entry [C1](/bali/visas/c1/) or eVOA.

For people who regularly travel around Asia, Europe or elsewhere, it is one of E33G's main advantages.

## How long does E33G last?

The initial ITAS is for **1 year**.

The official system provides for extension.

Requirements should be checked again before each extension, because rules and document requirements may change.

## When must you enter after issuance?

You must use E33G to enter within **90 days of issuance**.

The e-ITAS and re-entry permit are generated after you actually pass immigration control.

Do not start counting the ITAS period from the date the electronic visa is issued before you enter.

## Is E33G suitable for a family?

The principal E33G is issued to the remote worker.

A spouse and child receive their own dependent statuses.

The category depends on the relationship, documents and principal ITAS holder's status.

Read more: [E33G for a spouse and child](/bali/knowledge/e33g/family/).

## How is E33G different from C1?

The main distinction is the type of status.

### C1

- visitor visa;
- up to 180 days with extensions;
- one entry;
- leaving ends the current cycle.

### E33G

- one-year ITAS;
- for a remote worker of a foreign company;
- permits leaving and returning;
- better suited to genuine long-term life in Indonesia.

For a 2–4 month visit, E33G may be more than you need.

For a full year, provided you meet the requirements, it may be much more convenient.

## Banks and local documents

An ITAS generally gives a more practical basis for:

- banking matters;
- SKTT;
- vehicle registration;
- other local documents and services.

E33G does not mean a bank or government office will automatically approve an application.

Each organisation's requirements need to be checked separately.

## How long does SAFRWAY processing take?

SAFRWAY has two operational options.

### Standard

Approximately **7–10 working days**.

### Expedited

Approximately **5 working days**.

In rare cases, **6 working days**.

These are SAFRWAY estimates, not guaranteed dates of issue by Immigration.

## Why might the timeline change?

Immigration may:

- request another document;
- carry out additional checks;
- delay processing;
- experience a technical delay in a government system.

SAFRWAY does not control these processes and cannot guarantee that government delays will not occur.

The safest option is to **buy flights after the visa has actually been issued**.

If tickets are already booked or your situation is urgent, tell the manager before filing. Such cases are assessed individually.
