# Consular Legalization of Documents in Indonesia

## Quick answer

**Consular legalization may be necessary where an apostille is unavailable for the particular document and destination country.** The process can involve consecutive authentications of signatures and seals by competent Indonesian authorities, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Kemlu) and the destination country’s diplomatic mission. The steps depend on the document and the recipient’s requirements.

SAFRWAY helps identify the necessary sequence and coordinate qualified providers. We do not claim that every case requires every authority: first we establish which authentications are actually needed.

## When an apostille is not appropriate

- The destination country does not apply the Apostille Convention in the relevant relationship.
- The document falls within an excluded category or does not meet the competent authority’s conditions.
- The recipient legitimately requires a different permitted procedure under applicable rules.
- A special sector-specific, notarial or administrative action is needed and cannot be replaced by an apostille.

Even where a country participates in the Convention, **not all documents are covered**. Certain [commercial and corporate documents](/en/bali/documents/corporate/), in particular, need an individual assessment. Whether an [apostille](/en/bali/documents/apostille/) applies cannot be determined from the destination country alone.

## A possible route for an Indonesian document

The official Kemlu service for non-apostille legalization states that, after the relevant authentication by the Ministry of Law, an application for legalization may be submitted to Kemlu via **STEMPEL ASLI**. Depending on the destination country, further action by its embassy or consulate may be required.

**These stages are not universal.** Before accepting an order, we check the authorities involved, application method, required originals, representative’s powers and whether a personal visit is needed. Each ministry, notary and consulate acts within its own authority.

## What we check before processing

1. **Document origin:** where and by whom it was issued or signed, and whether it is an official or notarial document.
2. **Purpose:** the specific institution and destination country.
3. **Format:** original, notarized copy, official extract or other acceptable version.
4. **Existing certifications:** what has already been authenticated and whether it remains suitable for the next stage.
5. **Translation:** whether a [sworn translator](/en/bali/documents/certified-translations/) is needed, in which language and with what authentication process.
6. **Logistics:** who may submit, where the document is physically held and any shipping restrictions.

If the document originates abroad, the legalization chain may start **outside Indonesia**, at an authority in its country of origin.

## Frequently asked questions

### If a country has signed the Convention, is legalization never needed?

Not necessarily. Its applicability between the relevant countries and the type of document must be checked: exclusions and special procedures exist.

### Can an apostille replace consular legalization?

Only where the document and its intended use genuinely qualify for the apostille procedure. Our [comparison of the two processes](/en/bali/knowledge/documents/apostille-vs-legalization/) explains the difference.

### Does SAFRWAY apply a consular stamp itself?

No. Authentication is performed by the competent authority; SAFRWAY coordinates the agreed process.

### Do I need to visit the embassy in person?

That depends on the particular mission and the powers of the person submitting the documents. We check whether representation is possible in advance.

### Does legalization confirm that the contents of a contract are genuine?

In general, legalization authenticates the origin of signatures and seals, rather than verifying contractual terms or performance.

### Can documents of a foreign company be processed through Indonesia?

First, we check the issuing country, the document’s legal category and recipient requirements. Indonesia is not a universal legalization jurisdiction for all foreign documents.
